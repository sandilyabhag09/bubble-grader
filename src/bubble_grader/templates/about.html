About this site

What is Grader Form v2?

Grader Form v2 grades paper ACT practice tests for a small ACT tutoring program. Students turn in a photo of their filled-in answer sheet on Google Classroom, and their score comes back to them there.

Only the program's teachers sign in here. Students and parents never need an account on this site: students do everything in Google Classroom, the same way they would for any other assignment.

How it works

  1. Step 1

    The teacher assigns a practice test

    They create it from this site and pick which test it is. It shows up in Google Classroom like any other assignment.

  2. Step 2

    The student turns in a photo

    They fill in a paper ACT answer sheet, photograph or scan it, attach it and click Turn in.

  3. Step 3

    The app reads and scores it

    It lines the photo up with a blank answer sheet, finds each filled-in bubble, and checks it against the test's answer key.

  4. Step 4

    The score appears in Classroom

    Usually within minutes. A back-up check about once an hour picks up turn-ins it missed on recent assignments.

The app counts the correct answers in English, Math, Reading and Science, turns each count into a score from 1 to 36 using that practice test's own score chart, and averages the four into a composite. Phone photos work, including the HEIC format iPhones use; scanned PDFs work too (only the first page is read). It knows three answer-sheet layouts (the older ACT sheet, the 2025 ACT sheet, and the program's shorter version of it without trial-question rows), and if a student fills in a different one from the one the test expects, it tries the others.

What students get back

A grade on the assignment in Google Classroom. For a full practice test that's the composite score from 1 to 36; if the teacher assigned only one passage or a range of questions, it's based on how many of those questions were answered correctly.

If the teacher chooses, each student also gets an email, sent from the teacher's own address, with their scores (each section's score for a full test, or the passage score), the numbers of the questions they missed, and usually a marked-up copy of their own answer sheet:

Some newer ACT tests include trial questions that ACT doesn't count. On a full practice test they never count toward the score here either. If a student missed one, the email lists it separately as "not scored" so they can still learn from it.

When the app isn't sure

  • It won't grade a sheet it can't line up. If a photo can't be matched to a known answer sheet (a different form, too blurry, or too little of the sheet showing), no score is recorded. The app tries any other photos attached to the same turn-in; if none can be read, the teacher sees why and the student can turn in a clearer photo.
  • A partly cut-off photo is still graded. Questions missing from the photo earn no credit and are flagged for the teacher, so a photo of the whole sheet matters.
  • Blank and double-marked rows are flagged. A question read as blank or with more than one bubble filled earns no credit, and each one is listed for the teacher, usually with a close-up picture of that row. If one mark is clearly darker than another (say, a half-erased answer), the app takes the darker one.
  • Teachers can correct it. On a full practice test they can enter the answer the student meant, or fix any other question; the score is worked out again and the new grade can be sent to Classroom. So a grade can change after it first appears.

A clear, flat, in-focus photo of the whole sheet works best.

Is this AI?

No. Grading follows fixed rules: the app measures how dark each bubble is and compares the answers with the test's answer key. No AI model looks at students' sheets or decides their scores.

Who can use it, and what it keeps

Teachers only

Only teachers on the program's approved list can sign in. Anyone else is turned away and their sign-in is never saved.

Through the teacher's Google account

It sees the teacher's Classroom classes, class lists and turned-in work, reads only the files students attach, and sends email only when the teacher asks.

Photos are temporary

Full answer-sheet photos are deleted once the score emails go out, or automatically within 14 days. Small close-ups of flagged rows are kept with the grade. The original stays with the student's work in Classroom.

Scores are kept

Grades and question-by-question results are kept so teachers can look back at past tests. Students keep their grade in Classroom and any score email. Nothing is sold or shared with advertisers.

The privacy policy has the full details, including how to ask for data to be deleted.

Common questions

I'm a parent or student. Do I need to do anything on this site?

No. Students turn in a photo of their answer sheet in Google Classroom as usual, and the grade appears there. This site is for the program's teachers.

How soon does the score show up?

Usually within minutes of the turn-in. A back-up check about once an hour picks up recent turn-ins it missed, and teachers can grade any assignment by hand. If the photo can't be matched to an answer sheet at all, no score is posted, and the teacher sees that and can follow up.

What if a score looks wrong?

Tell the teacher. They can look at the marked-up sheet and the rows the app flagged, and fix the grade if it's wrong.

Do parents get the score email?

No. The app only ever emails the student, at the address on the Google Classroom class list, and only when the teacher sends it.

Why does Google's sign-in screen say "Bubble Grader"?

It's the same app. Google's sign-in screen and the privacy policy call it Bubble Grader; the top of this site says Grader Form v2.
